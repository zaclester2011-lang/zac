Scored entries rank first. Ten places carry a verified score because somebody went and measured them. Every other place carries an auto-drafted Discovery and no Colour at all — which is the state of every destination before the work is done, and the reason the index is worth building.

Colour: four checks on the ground, mean error 0.125 of a point — including one call made with no photograph of that water in existence, which landed exactly. Discovery: six corrections, six wrong, in both directions. The failure modes were scoring effort as obscurity, and scoring documentation as crowding. Neither is what Discovery measures.

Balos is where the automatic score fails, and the failure is instructive. Density sees empty land and calls it remote. It cannot see four hundred people off the Kissamos boats. The automatic score measures remoteness, not crowding — and closing that gap is exactly what a review is for.

The same query covers the whole world. Built from Overture Maps — the open map consortium — and Wikidata. Sulawesi was added by changing four numbers: one query returned 174,718 places in ninety seconds. The entire planet is 11.9 GB across sixteen files on a public bucket, free to anyone.

Photographs are the hard part. 104 places of 26,916 have a freely licensed photograph, because Wikidata only holds images of landmarks. No free source exists for an ordinary restaurant or hotel. The answer is the same as it is for Discovery: the people who go supply them.
