# Europe — Country-by-Country Water Body Audit

**Every European country, its best 10 water bodies, scored /30.**

Scoring: **Discovery · Colour · Water**, each /10.
**Weak-link rule: below 7 in any category is cut, however high the total.**

Started 26 September 2026. Working at 2–3 countries per round.
~44 countries to cover. Countries marked **DONE** below are researched;
everything else is outstanding.

**Europe's structural ceiling:** the Mediterranean is clear, not turquoise —
it goes turquoise only over white sand or limestone shallows. That caps
Colour at 8 almost everywhere, so a European 26 is rare and a 28 is close
to impossible outside a handful of Greek and Croatian shallows. Freshwater
is the reverse: Balkan and Alpine karst rivers hit Colour 9–10 but lose on
Water temperature (8–14°C).

**Spain's best three: Sa Calobra, Chorreras del Cabriel, Cala Coll Baix — all 23.**

**The two cuts are the interesting ones.** Cala de San Pedro has the
highest Discovery score in Spain (9 — no road, one hour on foot, a squatter
community at the end) and fails on Colour: Cabo de Gata's water is deep
blue, not turquoise. Cala Macarelleta is the exact inverse — Menorca's most
vivid turquoise, and Discovery 4 because it is on every list and reachable
by a paved path.

**Verdict: Spain is a 23 country.** No Spanish water body clears 24.
Chorreras del Cabriel is the one Colour 9, and it is a shallow river system —
Water 7 at best.

**Still to check:** Pozas de Melón and the Galician river pools, Aigüestortes
and the Pyrenean lakes (likely cut on temperature), Pozo de los Humos,
Ruta del Cares, Canary Islands (Charco Azul, Charco de los Clicos, Garachico),
Formentera, Cabrera archipelago.

**Greece's best: five entries tied at 24 — Voidomatis, Kleftiko, Seychelles
Beach, Glyka Nera, Neda.** That is the strongest field in Europe scored so far.

**Greece has Europe's crowding problem in its purest form.** Five of its
most vivid water bodies — Melissani, Balos, Navagio, Porto Katsiki,
Elafonissi — are cut on Discovery alone. Navagio scores Discovery 3: it is
the most photographed spot in the Mediterranean. Melissani has the only
Colour 10 in Europe found so far (sunlight through a collapsed cave roof
onto brackish water) and is a ticketed rowboat ride on a timetable.

**Elafonissi is also a Water cut at 6** — the pink shallows are ankle to
knee deep across most of their width. It is a wade, not a swim.

**The pattern: Greece's scoring entries are the ones nobody photographs.**
Neda (Discovery 9 — a gorge in the Peloponnese interior with no road to the
pools), Glyka Nera (reachable only on foot from Loutro or by boat, with
freshwater springs welling up through the seabed — hence the name), Ikaria's
Seychelles Beach, and the Voidomatis, which runs through the deepest gorge
in the world by depth-to-width ratio.

**Still to check:** Milos beyond Kleftiko (Sarakiniko, Tsigrado, Papafragas),
Egremni and Kathisma (Lefkada), Simos (Elafonisos), the Sporades
(Lalaria, Skopelos), Ionian sea caves (Papanikolis), Samaria and the Cretan
gorges, Lousios and the Arcadian rivers, Chalkidiki, Zagori's other rivers.

**Portugal's best: Poço Azul, Madeira — 23.** Nothing clears it.

**Portugal is the weakest major coastal country in Europe on these
criteria, and the reason is the Atlantic.** The mainland coast is
green-grey, cold (17–19°C even in August) and surf-driven. Colour 8 only
happens inland, in the *praias fluviais* — Portugal's genuinely distinctive
category, river beaches with communal barbecues, which score 21 across the
board: good on all three, outstanding on none.

**The Algarve is a Discovery graveyard.** Marinha and Benagil have Europe's
most photographed sea caves and score Discovery 3 — worse than Navagio.

**Loriga is the one real curiosity**: Portugal's only river beach in a
glacial valley, at 800 m in the Serra da Estrela.

**Still to check:** Madeira's levada pools in depth (25 Fontes, Risco),
Azores beyond São Miguel (Flores' Poço da Ribeira do Ferreiro, Faial),
Gerês / Peneda-Gerês cascades, Cascata da Pedra Furada, Alqueva.

**Cala Goloritzé at 25 is the highest-scoring water body in Europe found so
far** — and it held the same position on the old system at 48/48.

**Italy has Europe's second Colour 10, and cuts it twice.** Lampedusa's
Spiaggia dei Conigli is consistently ranked the best beach in the world
(Discovery 3). The Blue Grotto is a Colour 10 with **Water 3** — you lie
flat in a rowboat to get through the entrance and swimming is prohibited.
It is a light effect, not a swim.

**Gole dell'Alcantara is a Water cut at 5** — the Alcantara runs off Etna's
snowmelt at roughly 12°C year-round, and the gorge is knee-to-waist deep for
most of its walkable length. Waders are rented at the entrance. That is the
tell.

**Italy's real strength is that its top seven are all different things** —
a hike-in cove, inland jungle pools, a Sicilian canyon with staircase
descent, an island cove, a granite cove. No repetition problem, which is the
flaw flagged in the Sardinia–Corsica itinerary file.

**Still to check:** Cilento and the Campanian coast, Puglia (Grotta della
Poesia, Torre Sant'Andrea), Calabria's Costa Viola, the Aeolians
(Pollara, Cala Junco), Elba and the Tuscan archipelago, the Dolomite lakes
(Braies, Sorapis — likely Water cuts on temperature), Lake Garda's
Valle delle Cartiere, Marmore falls.

**Three French 25s — all Corsican — tying Cala Goloritzé for the European lead.**

**Plage de Ghignu scores Discovery 9**, the joint highest in Western Europe
with Neda and Cala de San Pedro: **no road access at all**, reached by boat
from Saint-Florent or a long 4x4 track. That is also exactly why the
Sardinia–Corsica file flags it as an open booking risk — the boats are
heavily seasonal.

**Verdon is the audit's most painful cut.** Colour 9 and Water 9 — Lac
d'Esparron reaches 26°C, the warmest freshwater in the survey — and
**Discovery 4**. It is one of Europe's premier canyons with pedal-boat hire
at the lake mouth and coach parking above it.

**Toulourenc fails on Water 6** and Restonica on Water 5: the Toulourenc is
a shallow river walk and the Restonica lakes sit at 1,700–1,900 m, meaning
snowmelt.

**Still to check:** Corsica's remaining pools (Tavignano, Bavella's other
branches), the Calanques beyond Cassis, Ardèche, Hérault and the
Languedoc rivers, Pyrenean lakes and the Cirque de Gavarnie (probable
temperature cuts), Réunion and the overseas départements if they count as
France for this purpose — noting Réunion's sea swimming is effectively
banned after fatal shark attacks.

**Expected shape of the answer before doing the work** — stated now so it
can be checked against the findings later:

- **Croatia, Slovenia, Bosnia and Montenegro** should produce Europe's
  highest Colour scores. Dinaric karst rivers (Soča, Krka, Una, Tara,
  Kravice) run 9–10 on Colour. They will be tested hardest on **Water
  temperature**, not colour.
- **Albania** should be Europe's Discovery leader — the Vjosa, the Blue Eye,
  Gjipe, and a coast that was closed to outsiders until the 1990s.
- **Iceland, Norway and the Faroes** will fail Water almost universally on
  temperature, with geothermal pools the exception.
- **The Netherlands, Belgium, Luxembourg, Denmark, Hungary, Moldova,
  Belarus and the micro-states** may not produce ten qualifying water
  bodies at all. Where a country cannot field ten, that will be recorded
  rather than padded.
