Why the line falls there

Three separate systems all peak north of the equator in August, and nothing equivalent runs in the south — the southern tropics are simply in winter.

What shuts the north

  • Atlantic hurricane seasonJun–Nov, peaks Aug–SepCaribbean, Bahamas, Yucatán, Florida coast, Belize
  • Southwest monsoonJun–SepWestern India, Maldives, Andaman coast, western Malaysia, Palawan
  • NW Pacific typhoonsJul–Oct, peaks Aug–SepOkinawa, Taiwan, Palau, central Vietnam, northern Philippines

What opens the south

  • Austral winterMay–OctThe South Pacific cyclone season is Nov–Apr — August is its opposite
  • Kusi, the SE monsoonJun–OctDry across East Africa. Cooler, breezier, but dry
  • The Australian dryMay–OctKimberley, Ningaloo, Queensland at their best

The three with a catch

  • Raja AmpatWind shift, rough seasNot rain — access. Wayag can be unreachable, and Wayag is the point
  • SeychellesSE tradesDry but persistently windy, and the sea is cooler
  • FloridaSprings vs coastThe springs hold 20–22°C all year and don't care. The coast is in hurricane season

Niue is not on this chart, though its water is the clearest on earth. Air New Zealand drops to one flight a week from 19 October 2026 — Saturdays only, from Auckland. A trip there can be exactly seven days, or fourteen, and nothing else.

Amber is not a soft red. Raja Ampat in August is dry and warm; the problem is that the wind shifts and the sea gets up, so boats can't reach the far islands. You would get there and not get to Wayag.
