Before you book anything

  1. Confirm the Kendwa full moon party runs Saturday 24. The moon is full on Monday 26. If the party is on the 31st instead, the trip restructures.
  2. Chumbe. Fourteen places, one 10:00 sailing, Thursday 29. Book first, book early.
  3. Tide tables for 30 October and 31 October — Nakupenda needs low water on the Friday, and Mtende needs whichever state the fishermen at Jambiani tell you. Sunday's Pungume needs none, which is why it sits on the weakest tide of the trip.
  4. Niamembe, through Eco & Culture Tours from Unguja Ukuu. $95 each for three, barbecue included. Ask them whether the cave walk works on 1 November — it needs low water and that is the trip's weakest tide.
  5. Ask for Niamembe by name, not "a Menai Bay trip." The default product from Fumba is Safari Blue, which goes to Kwale with a crowd and never comes near the southern islets.
  6. Tumbatu. A local guide, and permission sought from the elders in advance.
  7. Ask the Pemba driver whether he'll do the Kigomasha tracks after sunset.
  8. Airport taxi for Monday 2 November, 03:50 — pre-booked, not hoped for.

On the numbers. Eleven of the twelve water bodies here were researched against multiple independent sources. Niamembe is the exception — its 27 rests on one operator's page and one aggregated line. It scores highest of anything in Menai Bay and it is the least proven thing on the itinerary, which is a real trade and worth naming.

Research moved seven scores and removed five stops. Vundwe fell from 27 to 20 and left entirely — coral rag forest and mangrove, not a beach. The Kwale lagoon fell to 24 once it emerged that no source anywhere states its size. Pungume and Nakupenda each dropped a point, on being a published excursion and on afternoon crowds. Vumawimbi rose to carry Tuesday outright; Michamvi Kae rose on the one fact that matters on that coast, its tide barely moving; and Niamembe rose from a guessed 24 to a researched 27.

Of the ten Menai Bay islands, four failed the weak-link rule outright. Pamunda and Sume have seaweed-farming Eucheuma growing around them — the same thing that cut Uzi. Ukombe's reefs are being broken by octopus fishermen. Tele is jagged coral rag with no beach. All four had been sitting at a hopeful ~24 on nothing but the group's reputation for being undisturbed — the identical mistake that produced Vundwe's 27.

Five of eight days involve a boat, down from six. Nine of twelve water bodies are salt water over a coral platform, and that is the honest limit of this trip: it is one idea done very well in many places, not many ideas. Kuza Cave is the only water body here that isn't the sea — a freshwater sinkhole under an open roof. Niamembe's cave on the last day is the only other thing on the itinerary that isn't a beach, a reef or a forest, which is a large part of why it took Sunday from a better sandbank with better snorkelling.
