Forest, cave, cove

26 / 30
Inland · by road · tide-sensitive
  • 09:30Jozani Forest, 2 hours — Zanzibar red colobus, found nowhere else on earth, plus the mangrove boardwalk over Chwaka Bay.—
  • 12:30Kuza Cave, Jambiani A 50-metre limestone sinkhole, spring-fed, about three metres deep, turquoise under an open roof. Sacred to the village, who call it Panga na Maji — the cave with water. The only freshwater on the whole trip, and the one thing here that isn't sea over coral.27
  • 15:30Mtende Beach — a cove cut into limestone cliffs on the south-east corner, well off the tourist strip.25
  • 19:00Back.
Mtende's tide is genuinely unresolvedSources contradict each other outright — one says the sheltered lagoon forms at low water and swimming is unsafe at high, another says the cove is calm and swimmable at high and the sea retreats hundreds of metres at low. Ask in Jambiani that morning and be ready to swap Kuza and Mtende around. Reef shoes either way — coral and sea urchins.

Before you book anything

  1. Confirm the Kendwa full moon party runs Saturday 24. The moon is full on Monday 26. If the party is on the 31st instead, the trip restructures.
  2. Chumbe. Fourteen places, one 10:00 sailing, Thursday 29. Book first, book early.
  3. Fumba tide tables, 28 October and 1 November. High water between 13:00 and 17:00 decides which day gets the mangrove lagoon; Pungume takes the other.
  4. Both Fumba dhows from one skipper, negotiated together. Direct at the jetty is £80–140 against £150–250 through a hotel — and two days is real leverage. He'll also know the tide better than any website, and which islet is worth stopping at that week.
  5. Confirm the Sunday charter actually reaches Pungume — 45 minutes beyond where scheduled boats stop. They will offer Kwale instead.
  6. Tumbatu. A local guide, and permission sought from the elders in advance.
  7. Ask the Pemba driver whether he'll do the Kigomasha tracks after sunset.
  8. Airport taxi for Monday 2 November, 03:50 — pre-booked, not hoped for.

On the numbers. Ten of the thirteen water bodies here were researched against multiple independent sources. One — the optional Menai islet — rests on a Wikipedia stub and a single sentence about the southern islets being undisturbed. It is marked provisional and it is optional for that reason.

Research moved four scores. Vundwe fell from 27 to 20 and left the itinerary entirely. Pungume dropped a point once it turned out to be a published $55 excursion from Kizimkazi. Nakupenda dropped one on afternoon crowds — which is why it now goes first. Vumawimbi rose to carry Tuesday outright. Two more stops, the Menai reef and Chapwani, were cut to give better water more time.

Six of eight days involve a boat. Ten of thirteen water bodies are salt water over a coral platform. The alternation is as good as the fixed points allow — island, land, hop, island, hop, inland, island, with no two hops consecutive — but this is a boat-heavy trip by design. Kuza Cave and the mangrove lagoon are what stop it being a single idea repeated eight times, and both sit in the back half deliberately.
