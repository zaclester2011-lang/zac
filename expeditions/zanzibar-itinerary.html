The scoring

Three things out of ten each, and a rule that cuts the weak link

Discovery — does it feel like you found it. Colour — turquoise or emerald, vivid either way. Water — how much of it, and how swimmable. A puddle is 1; a proper swim is 10.

Anything scoring below 7 in any category is cut regardless of its total. That's what removed Mnemba on discovery, the whale sharks on discovery and colour, and every tidal east-coast beach on water.

Confidence isn't even. Misali, Vumawimbi, Kuza, Chumbe, Bawe, Nakupenda, Tumbatu, Mtende and the Kwale lagoon were researched properly. Pungume, Vundwe and the Menai islets weren't — those rest on very little, and Pungume and Vundwe carry Friday between them.

Before you go

Eight things to sort

  • Chumbe — book first. Fixed sailing, capped numbers, and the least predictable price
  • Tide tables for 28 October (high), 31 October and 1 November (both low). Jambiani station for Mtende, Stone Town station for Nakupenda
  • Private dhow at Fumba for both Wednesday and Friday — booked at the jetty, not through the hotel. Hotel bookings carry 40–50% markups, and that gap is worth £300–400 across the trip
  • Tumbatu guide and permission
  • Stone Town room from 22 October so it's ready at 07:50
  • A driver, not a hire car — six days. On six of eight days a car would sit at a jetty, and he's the one who knows which boat owner to talk to
  • Airport taxi for 03:50 on 2 November, pre-booked
  • Snorkel gear from home — six snorkelling days, and rental is £5–10 a head a day
A note on the numbers

£4,200 is the working figure — £3,000 of it already booked for flights, hotels and the two Pemba hops. The rest is boats, a driver for six days, visas and the party. Booking the dhows directly at the jetty rather than through a hotel is the single biggest lever on what's left.

Sunset is 18:25 every day and barely shifts — Zanzibar sits six degrees south. Stone Town faces west, so Forodhani works on any evening you're back in time.
