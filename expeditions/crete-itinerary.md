### Why it is ordered this way

**The 13:00 arrival buys Seitan Limania for nothing.** Chania airport and the
beach are both on Akrotiri, so you go from the hire desk to the water, not out
from the hotel later in the week.

**The charter goes Tuesday with Saturday as the retry.** It is not a season
question — every operator says *"April to October, when the sea conditions
allow"*, so **31 October is inside its window.** That puts four days of slack
behind the most fragile entry in the plan. **Domata has no usable land route:**
the E4 there is graded Δύσκολη, climbs to ~700 m, is called one of the hardest
sections in Crete, and its own recommended season is April–September.

**Gavdos goes Thursday–Friday** because the Chora Sfakion sailings are Thu–Sun,
and that keeps Saturday free for the retry.

**The eastward drift on 1–2 November** turns the two post-ferry days into

## ON THE GROUND

**Taxis:** no ride-hailing app covers western Crete — **no Uber, no FREENOW,
no Bolt.** **Chania radio dispatch: Taxi 18300.** FREENOW works in Heraklion
only, useful on the last morning.

**Apps:** **Windy** *(check it the night before every boat day — it is what the
boatmen are looking at)* · **Poseidon System, poseidon.hcmr.gr** *(the official
marine forecast; wave height is what cancels a sailing)* · **meteo.gr** ·
**WhatsApp and Viber** *(a Greek boatman in October answers Viber, not email)* ·
**Google Maps with Crete downloaded offline** *(signal drops on the south coast
and in the gorges)*.

**Weather watch, specific to these entries:** **Domata, Trypiti, Sentoni and
Karoumes are all gorge mouths.** **After heavy rain, runoff can cloud the water
at the outflow — so check the rain forecast, not just the wind.** A wet 48
hours before the charter could spoil three of them at once.

**Elafonissi rules** *(1 km from Kedrodasos)*: **park 500–600 m out, stay
outside the fenced vegetation, and taking sand is illegal under Law 1650/86.**
The pink is only at the waterline, and it is fading because people take it.

## 🔎 SENTONI VERIFIED IN GREEK — 25 → 24

**Asked directly: is Sentoni turquoise? No source says so.** Sentoni was the
tenth entry carrying an unverified score, and like the nine before it, it fell
when read in Greek.

| Source | What it actually says |
|---|---|
| Elizabeth Estate Agency | *«Η όμορφη παραλία Σεντόνι διαθέτει **βότσαλο** και **βαθιά καταγάλανα** νερά»* — pebble, and **deep, intensely blue** water |
| thisiscrete.gr | *«**βαθιά, κρυστάλλινα** νερά του Λιβυκού Πελάγους»*, below *«απόκρημνους βράχους και ψηλά πρανή»* — deep, crystalline; sheer rock and high scree slopes |
| cretanbeaches.gr | Pebble; **the neighbouring Trypiti's bottom is rocky** |

**The word is καταγάλανα, not τιρκουάζ.** Greek has a specific word for
turquoise water and uses it freely — Cape Tripiti on Gavdos got its 25 on
exactly that word. **Nobody applies it to Sentoni.** What they apply is
*βαθιά* — deep — three times over.

**That is the mechanism, not a quibble.** Turquoise is shallow water over pale
rock. Sentoni is **deep water over pebble and rock, under sheer cliffs** — the
same profile that took Delfinia from 26 to 21 and Sougia's Trypiti from 26 to
25. Deep clear water off a pebble shore reads navy and bottle-green, which is
beautiful and is not what this trip is scored on.

**Colour 8 → 7. 9·7·8 = 24.** It survives the weak-link rule by exactly one
point.

## ↩️ DOMATA RESTORED TO 26 — the murk is seasonal and I cut it out of season

**Zac was right.** The photographs disagree with each other — turquoise in
some, cobalt in others — and the reason is the time of year. **I penalised
Domata's Colour for a seasonal defect without checking the season.**

### The inconsistency was mine

Earlier in this audit, **Discovery scores penalised for crowding were all
re-scored upward**, because crowding is seasonal and late October empties out.
Seitan Limania went 24 → 25 on exactly that reasoning, Agia Roumeli 23 → 24,
Balos 22 → 23–24.

**The murk at Domata is seasonal in precisely the same way, and I did not apply
the same correction.** One filter, applied to one category and not the other.

### Why late October is the best case, not the worst

The murk is not dirt and not sediment. Every source traces it to one cause:
**«υπόγειων πηγών γλυκού νερού»** — *underground freshwater springs.* It is a
freshwater lens hazing the saltwater.

**Crete's rain falls between roughly October and April. May to September is
effectively rainless.** Karst spring discharge follows that recharge with a
lag, so **the springs are at or near their annual minimum at the end of the dry
season — which is exactly your window.** You arrive on 26 October, before the
autumn rains have put anything back into the Lefka Ori aquifer.

**The weakest freshwater plume of the year.** *(Reasoned from Crete's rainfall
pattern — I could not find a published discharge figure for these particular
springs, so this is mechanism, not measurement.)*

### The murk is also one source, repeated

| Source | Water |
|---|---|
| neakriti.gr | *«καταγάλανα νερά»* **in its own headline** — the murk is a line in the body |
| ekriti.gr | *«Η ειδυλλιακή παραλία των Σφακίων με τα **καταγάλανα** νερά»* |
| travel.gr | *«**γαλανά** νερά»* |
| elizabethestateagency | *transparent, deep-blue* |
| cretanbeaches | *«καταγάλανα… **συνήθως όμως θολά**»*; colour field **«Βαθύ μπλε»** |

**Two sites mention murk. Four put clear blue water in the headline.** I had
built a cut on the minority reading of one line.

### Restored

**Colour 6 → 8. Water 7 → 8. `10·8·8 = 26`.**

**Colour stays at 8, not 9, for the one reason that is not seasonal: the
substrate is grey.** *Fine pebbles in tones of grey*, *grey sand.* Grey is
pale enough for turquoise in shallow clear water — it is far from basalt — but
nobody has written **τιρκουάζ** about Domata, so 8 remains the honest figure.

*For scale: Marmara holds Colour 9 with «fine **dark** pebbles», because the
turquoise there is sourced to the white limestone cliffs above it. Domata sits
under the same Lefka Ori limestone. **8 may be the conservative call.***

> ### Europe's only possible 27 is back open
> Discovery 10 is sourced and unarguable. Water 8 is standard for the Libyan
> Sea in late October. **Colour is the one open figure in the entire archive,
> and you settle it by standing in it.**

### What this does NOT rescue

**Potamos stays cut.** Its problem is **orange sand from clay** — a property of
the rock, not the calendar — and *«συχνά κύμα λόγω του προσανατολισμού της»*,
frequent waves from its orientation, which if anything get **worse** later in
the autumn. Nothing seasonal to credit.

**Preveli stays at 24.** It fell on **Colour** — *γαλαζοπράσινα*, never
τιρκουάζ — and that is not a seasonal reading either.

## 🔴 RED BEACH / Κόκκινη Άμμος, Matala — 23 → 21

**The dark-substrate rule, stated out loud by the source itself.**

> *«Χαρακτηριστικό της… είναι η αφράτη **κεραμιδοκόκκινη άμμος** που προέρχεται
> από τα κοκκινωπά εδάφη της περιοχής»*
>
> *«Τα χρώματα της θάλασσας, **λόγω της άμμου**, αποκτούν ένα υπέροχο
> **πρασινογάλαζο** χρώμα»*

**Terracotta-red sand from the region's red soils — and the sea takes its
green-blue colour BECAUSE OF that sand.** The causal chain written down:
*substrate → water colour.*

This is the rule that cut **Aspes** (*the black beach of the Asterousia*),
**Pyrgos** and **Potamos** on Gavdos, and **Seixal** on Madeira. Everywhere
else I inferred it. **Here the guide states it.** Red sand gives
*πρασινογάλαζο* — green-blue, olive-tinged. Genuinely beautiful. **Not
turquoise, and it cannot be**, because turquoise needs a pale floor.

### The other two scores

**Discovery 7.** It is **800 m from Matala**, one of the most famous beaches in
Crete. 15–25 min over the Kastri hill with some scrambling and a steep final
descent — a real little walk — **but there are umbrellas and a canteen on the
sand**, a boat runs from Matala harbour, and protothema lists it among
*«τις έξι καλύτερες παραλίες γυμνιστών στον κόσμο»* — **the six best nudist
beaches in the world.** Nothing on that list is hidden.

**Water 7.** *«ευάλωτη στους **δυτικούς ανέμους** που πνέουν **συχνά** στην
περιοχή»* — exposed to the west winds, which blow often. Wind-driven chop over
fine red sand is the sediment problem again.

**`7·7·7 = 21.`** It clears the weak-link rule with nothing to spare.

### Consequence

**Monday 2 November is Agiofarago 24, outright.** Red Beach was carried in the
itinerary as the alternative on that day; at 21 against 24 it is not a
contender. **A 3 km gorge walk ending at the Goumenospilio cave beats a
famous nudist beach with a canteen, by three points.**

*Keep Red Beach as a 20-minute curiosity if you are standing in Matala anyway —
the colour is worth seeing precisely because it is the wrong colour.*

## 🪨 THE REAL FINDING: IN CRETE, TURQUOISE AND DISCOVERY CANNOT COINCIDE

**This is not bad luck in the sources. It is geology, and it explains every
result above at once.**

**Crete's roadless south coast is limestone cliff falling straight into deep
water, with pebble shores.** No road reaches it *because* the mountains drop
into the sea — which is the same fact that makes the water **deep**. Deep water
over pebble reads **Βαθύ μπλε**. Every single entry: Domata, Marmara, Trypiti,
Sentoni, Agiofarago, Lakkoudi, Lykos, Agia Roumeli, Loutro. **All Βαθύ μπλε or
Γαλάζιο, all βαθιά.**

**Crete's turquoise is in shallow white-sand lagoons in the far west** —
**Elafonissi, Balos, Kedrodasos** — which exist precisely because the land is
*low and flat* there. **And low flat land is where roads go.** Car parks, tour
boats, Discovery 5–8.

> **The thing that makes a beach unreachable is the same thing that makes its
> water deep blue. The thing that makes water turquoise is the same thing that
> lets a road reach it.**

**This is the Europe-wide finding — *Colour 9–10 behind Discovery 9–10 never
coincide* — now proven inside a single island, with a mechanism.** It is not a
sourcing problem that more searching would fix.

# 🍂 THE LATE-OCTOBER AUDIT — what I applied, and what I missed

**Zac asked whether the dates are built in: colour changing, and the place
being far emptier. Partly. And I applied the seasonal argument in one direction
only, which was wrong.**

## ✅ What was correctly applied

**Crowding is seasonal, so Discovery was re-scored for an empty week.**
Seitan Limania, Agia Roumeli and Balos all had summer-crowd penalties removed
earlier. **Permanent access difficulty stays; crowding does not.**

**Freshwater springs are at their annual minimum** at the end of a rainless
May–September, so the murk at Domata, Glyka Nera, Agios Antonios and Ilingas is
at its weakest of the year. **And the Water gate now handles that properly.**

**Boat traffic has stopped**, which removes the churned-sediment and
diesel-sheen complaints at Preveli and Balos.

## ❌ WHAT I MISSED: the sun is low, and that works against turquoise

**Crete sits at 35°N. Midday sun elevation:**

| | Solar elevation at noon |
|---|---|
| Late June | **≈ 78°** |
| **Late October** | **≈ 42°** |

**Turquoise is light that has gone down through the water, bounced off a pale
floor and come back up.** At 42° a far greater share of the light **reflects off
the surface** instead of entering it, and what does enter travels a longer
slant path. **Less bottom-return means less turquoise and more mirror.**

**And every structured «Χρώμα νερού» field was almost certainly set from a
summer photograph — high sun, maximum colour.** So the fields are the *best*
case, not the average.

> **This cuts against my own Domata argument.** I used seasonality to *restore*
> Domata's Colour and never asked whether the same season dims it. **Both are
> true: the springs run weakest and the sun sits lowest.** They partly cancel.
> **Domata holds at 8 — but as a genuinely uncertain 8, not a quietly
> optimistic one.**

### Worse for the cliff beaches specifically

**A 42° sun cannot clear a 600–700 m cliff for long.** Trypiti is already
described as **cliff-shaded much of the day** — in late October that shadow is
longer and arrives earlier. **The same applies to Domata, Marmara, Lykos and
Glyka Nera**, all of which sit under big south-coast walls.

### And the sea is rougher, not calmer

**Late October is windier than summer**, which works against the entries that
already take wave: **Agios Pavlos** *(«ισχυρό κύμα λόγω των ανέμων»)* ·
**Fetifes** · **Red Beach** *(west winds)* · **Lissos** *(southerlies)* ·
**Potamos** *(«συχνά κύμα»)*. **Wind-stirred fine sediment is the main colour
risk of your week — not temperature.**

## 🥾 THE ALTERNATIVES, AND WHY THEY LOSE

**① All the way on foot from Sougia — 4–5 hrs EACH WAY.**
8–10 hours of walking in a **9h50 daylight window** (sunrise ~07:30, sunset

round trip.** The killer is the last stretch: Greek sources call
**Poikilassos → Domata *«από τα πιο κοπιαστικά και δύσκολα κομμάτια στην
Κρήτη»*** — a 25-minute descent to the Tripiti gorge bed, a steep crossing, and
*«αχνό μονοπάτι που ανεβαίνει προς το χείλος της απότομης πλαγιάς»*: an
indistinct path climbing toward the rim.

**② From Agia Roumeli.** The daily ANENDYK ferry reaches Agia Roumeli, and

**③ Down the Tripiti gorge — ruled out.** *«Κανένας δρόμος δεν φτάνει στην
είσοδο του φαραγγιού»*. Access is **3 hrs from Omalos up a near-vertical
Gigilos climb**, then **8½–9 hours of descent** — **normally done over two
days**, camping at the col or the ruined mitato of Tzatzimos at 1,480 m.
**No water in the gorge**, a dangerous entrance passage, and the **Tsounoplaka
crossing *"which needs a guide or local knowledge."***

**④ Down the Klados gorge — ruled out.** *«Το πιο αδιάβατο και άγριο φαράγγι
της Κρήτης»* — ropes and a guide, **42 waterfalls**, 12–14 hrs from Kokkinovari
*(itself 5 hrs from Omalos)*, 13–20 hrs from Omalos. **Not on the mountaineering
clubs' programmes.**

## ❌ WHY NOT FOR DOMATA

trip, which is exactly why its Discovery is 10.**

**2 · A 30 HP boat has almost no sea-state tolerance.** The whole plan already
turns on **Windy and the Poseidon System** because **wave height is what
cancels a sailing.** Late October brings afternoon wind to this coast. A caïque
copes; a small rental does not.

**3 · Landing at Domata is a beach landing through swell onto pebble.** No
jetty, no quay. **Local skippers do it routinely. A first-timer risks the boat,
and the deposit.**

**4 · The boatman's judgement is most of what you are buying.** A Sougia
skipper **will refuse to go** when the sea is wrong, and he is right. **Rent
the boat yourself and that decision becomes James's** — on unfamiliar water,
unlicensed, with a 15-year-old and a 13-year-old aboard, on the one day the
whole trip is built around. **For €60–100, the skipper is the cheapest thing in
the plan.**

**5 · Availability is unconfirmed.** The self-drive service is advertised as
daily, but **nothing confirms it runs in late October.**

# 🔆 THE DARK-SUBSTRATE RULE WAS MEASURING THE WRONG THING

**Zac has now said twice that Aspes, Skiadaki and Mavri Paralia are turquoise.
He has looked at them. I was reading the word for the sand. Those are not the
same measurement, and the distinction is the whole error.**

## What actually sets water colour

**The SUBMERGED floor — not the beach above the waterline.**

**My rule has been reading «μαύρη άμμος» on the shore and deducting from the
water.** But a beach's material and its seabed are two different surfaces.
**The Asterousia is LIMESTONE country** — that is why its cliffs collapse into
caves with *rectangular* sides, and why **«Άσπες» means landslide.** **Dark
coarse sand at the waterline is collapsed cliff debris sitting in front of a
pale limestone shelf.**

### The distinction I should have been drawing

| | |
|---|---|
| **Kills turquoise** | **Clay and soil** — they enter the water column and stay suspended. **Potamos** *(«πορτοκαλί λόγω του αργίλου»)*, **Stavrolimni** *(a stream carrying clay)*, **Red Beach** *(«κεραμιδοκόκκινη άμμος» from the region's red SOILS — and its own page states the causal chain)* |
| **Does NOT** | **Coarse rock fragments on a pale shelf.** Black pebble lies still. **The water above a limestone floor stays turquoise, and the dark beach makes it look MORE vivid, not less** |

**And Mavri Paralia's own headline says exactly this, which I dismissed as
vague:** **«Μαύρη άμμος, σπηλιές και ΧΡΩΜΑΤΙΣΤΑ νερά»** — black sand, caves,
and **COLOURED water.** *That is precisely how you would describe vivid
turquoise seen against black sand. The contrast is the point of the sentence.*

**I also have to withdraw the line I wrote one section ago** — *"two of the
three Discovery 10s are black sand and cannot be turquoise."* **That was the
error stated as a conclusion.**

# 🔵 ΒΟΥΛΟΛΙΜΝΗ REGRADED — **7 · 8 · 10 = 25**

**Zac has looked at it: «rocks creating a HALF turquoise/light blue circle.»**

## That observation is self-confirming

**"Half" is the tell.** The sources say the pool **«δεν έχει παραλία»** — no
beach — and **the seabed drops off steeply**, with the bottom described as
**rocky OR sandy**, which I read as sources disagreeing.

**They were not disagreeing. They were describing different halves of the same
pool.** **The shallow sandy half reads turquoise; the deep half reads blue.**
A crater joined to the sea by a narrow opening will have exactly that
geometry — a shelf on one side, depth on the other.

**Zac's photograph and the Greek text agree completely once you stop treating
the pool as uniform.**

**Colour 7 → 8.** *Not 9 — by his own account only half of it reads turquoise.*
