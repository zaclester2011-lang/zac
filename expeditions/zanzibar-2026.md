## KEY DETAILS PER DAY

**Tumbatu** — boat from Mkokotoni, 30 min crossing. Third-largest island in the archipelago, almost no tourism. Secluded beaches, Jongowe medieval Swahili ruins, island lighthouse. **Needs a local guide and permission** — the Watumbatu keep to themselves. Mkokotoni is 30 min from Kendwa.

**Misali** — boat from Wesha, 45 min. Uninhabited islet in a marine conservation area. 300+ coral and marine species. Snorkel straight off the beach. Shallows at 5m turquoise dropping to 40m indigo — you can see the shelf end from the surface.

**Vumawimbi** — 3km of untouched white sand, Ngezi rainforest to the waterline, frequently deserted. Then Ngezi Forest (1h) and Ras Kigomasha — Pemba's northern tip, 1900 British lighthouse, Pemba Channel (2,000m deep) meeting open ocean.

**Michamvi Kae** — west-facing side of the Michamvi peninsula. **One of the few Zanzibar beaches where the tide barely fluctuates.** Calm, safe swimming. Boats out for reef, starfish, clams. From the rugged tip: sunrise over the Indian Ocean, sunset over Chwaka Bay. **The only sunset over water in the trip.**

**Fumba** — dhow from Fumba into Menai Bay, Zanzibar's largest marine protected area (470 km²). **Pungume is 45 min south — the far end of the bay.** Third stop: ask the skipper which islet is best that week (Miwi, Niamembe, Pamunda, Sume). Kwale is the fallback but it's the Safari Blue hub and therefore the busiest.

**Jozani + Kuza + Mtende** — Jozani for the Zanzibar red colobus (found nowhere else on earth) and the mangrove boardwalk. Kuza is a sacred limestone jungle cave with a 50m turquoise pool, 3m deep, carved over 250,000 years, community-run. Mtende is a cove between two rock formations with ancient trees above it.

**Chumbe** — Tanzania's first Marine National Park, protected since 1994, **visitor numbers strictly capped**. Reef, forest trail, intertidal pools. Fixed sailing from Mbweni.

**Stone Town hop** — **no driving at all**, walk to the harbour. Bawe is 45 min west, far enough that few bother — 550m beach and reef off the sand. Nakupenda rises out of open sea at low tide with no land in any direction. Chapwani is a short hop back.
