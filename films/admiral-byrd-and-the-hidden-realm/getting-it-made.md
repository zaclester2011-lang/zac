# Getting the Film Made (and onto Netflix, Disney+, Amazon, Sky…)

This is an honest guide to how a script becomes a film on a streaming service. Every film starts with a script, and you now have a first draft. Getting it all the way to a screen is a long road, but it's a real one.

---

## How streaming services actually work

**1. You can't upload a film to Netflix, Disney+, Amazon Prime Video, Sky or Discovery yourself.** They aren't like YouTube. They **buy** finished films, or **pay** production companies to make them.

**2. They don't accept scripts from the public.** Netflix, Disney, Amazon and Sky all refuse "unsolicited submissions." If you email them a script, it gets deleted unread, for legal reasons. They only accept projects through:
- a literary **agent** or **manager**
- an **entertainment lawyer**
- an established **producer** or production company they already work with

**3. A film usually goes to one platform, not all of them.** Deals are normally **exclusive**. A Netflix film stays on Netflix, and Disney+ mostly shows films Disney owns. What *does* happen:
- **Different countries, different platforms.** A film can be on one service in the US and on Sky in the UK.
- **Different times, different platforms.** A film can play in cinemas first, then on one streamer, and years later on others.

**4. Discovery makes documentaries**, not fiction films. See the companion documentary idea in [`pitch.md`](pitch.md).

## The realistic path, step by step

### Step 1: Make the script yours
- **Rewrite it in your own words.** This draft was written with an AI (Claude) from your story idea. That matters for three reasons:
  - **Copyright:** in the US, copyright protects work written by *humans*. Text written purely by an AI isn't protected the same way, but your own rewriting, changes and additions are.
  - **Honesty:** some competitions and buyers ask whether AI was used. Answer truthfully.
  - **It's your film.** Change anything you want: names, scenes, jokes, the ending.
- **Read real screenplays** of films you love. Many are published online and in books.
- **Get feedback** from people who will be honest with you, then revise. Professional scripts go through many drafts.

### Step 2: Protect your work
- **Keep dated copies of every draft.** This GitHub repository already does that: every commit is a dated record.
- **US:** you can register the script with the U.S. Copyright Office, and/or the **WGA Script Registry** (open to non-members). It gives you dated proof of authorship.
- **UK:** copyright is automatic. There's no official registry, so keep your dated drafts.

### Step 3: Build a pitch package
- **Logline, one-page synopsis, character list.** These are in [`pitch.md`](pitch.md).
- **A pitch deck.** 10–15 slides with mood images, characters and the world.
- **Concept art or a mood board.** Artwork of the Veil, Solace and the Ilun sells a big fantasy world faster than words.

### Step 4: Make something people can watch
A short film or "proof of concept" is the best calling card a new filmmaker can have. Some scenes here are cheap to shoot:

| Scene | What you'd need |
|---|---|
| **The press conference** (the ending) | One room, a podium, old-fashioned suits and hats, camera flashes. Very doable. |
| **The planning scenes** (the opening) | A desk, maps, a 1940s look. |
| **The hotel scene with Marie** | Two actors, one room. Emotional and simple. |
| **Through the Veil / the hidden realm** | Needs VFX or animation. Try an **animatic** (moving storyboards with sound) or a short animated test. |

Put it online and enter it into **film festivals**. Check age rules: there are festivals and competitions just for young filmmakers.

### Step 5: Get the script seen
- **Screenplay competitions and fellowships.** Well-known ones include the Austin Film Festival Screenplay Competition and the Academy Nicholl Fellowships. Check each one's rules, including age requirements.
- **The Black List** website, where industry readers browse scripts (paid).
- **UK schemes:** the BBC Writersroom runs open calls for new writers.
- **Film school and courses** help you build skills and contacts.

### Step 6: Representation, then producers, then platforms
A strong script, a competition placing, or a well-received short helps you get a **manager or agent**. They take the script to **producers**, who take it to studios and streamers. When a film is finished, **sales agents** sell it to platforms around the world.

## Things producers will ask about this film

- **Budget.** As live action, this is a big VFX film. Studios rarely buy that size of film from a brand-new writer, though great scripts do sell. A cheaper version is easier to get made first: animation, a limited series, or a smaller "found footage" take.
- **Real people.** Admiral Byrd (who died in 1957) and his wife Marie are real historical figures. Films about real people are common, but a studio's lawyers will check the script (a "clearance" review). The script makes clear that the story is **fiction inspired by a legend**, and the "lost diary" is widely regarded as a hoax. Keep presenting it that way.
- **Original world.** The Ilun, Solace, the Veil and the Garden of the Lost are original creations. They don't copy the beings from the legend, so the world is fully yours to build on.

## Real history vs. legend

These parts of the film are **real**:
- **Operation Highjump** (1946–47) was a giant US Navy Antarctic expedition, with Byrd as officer in charge.
- On **January 29, 1947**, an R4D transport carrying Byrd took off from the deck of the aircraft carrier **USS *Philippine Sea*** using JATO rocket bottles, then flew on to **Little America IV**.
- Highjump fliers found an **ice-free "oasis"** with open lakes on the Antarctic coast. Contemporary films called it a "Shangri-La." (It's mentioned on the radio in the script.)
- In **1934**, Byrd nearly died of carbon monoxide poisoning alone at **Advance Base**. He wrote about it in his book *Alone* (1938).
- Byrd claimed the first flight over the **North Pole** (1926). Historians still debate whether he really reached it.
- **Operation Crossroads**: two atomic tests at Bikini Atoll in July 1946.
- **Byrd's dog Igloo**, a fox terrier, went on his polar expeditions.
- **Extinct species** in the Garden: the passenger pigeon (the last one, Martha, died in 1914), the thylacine (the last known one died in 1936), the great auk, the dodo and Steller's sea cow.
- **The Antarctic Treaty** (signed 1959) sets Antarctica aside for peace and science and bans military bases and nuclear explosions there.
- **"Shenandoah"** is a traditional American folk song (public domain). Byrd was born in Winchester, Virginia, in the Shenandoah Valley.

These parts are **invented**:
- The hole, the Veil, the hidden realm, the Ilun, the secret flight, the Pentagon threat, and everything Byrd says at the press conference.
- The "lost diary" that inspired the legend is widely regarded as a **hoax**. It first circulated decades after Byrd's death.
- Evelyn, Frank, Eddie, Stiles, Prescott and the reporters are **fictional characters**.
