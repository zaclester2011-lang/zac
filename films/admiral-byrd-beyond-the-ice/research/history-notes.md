# History notes: the real story behind the film

*Research notes for ADMIRAL BYRD: BEYOND THE ICE. Setting decision: the film stays in **Antarctica**.*

## Why Byrd really went to Antarctica in 1946–47

Operation Highjump was a giant US Navy expedition, and its main purpose was **military training**. World War II had just ended and the Cold War with the Soviet Union was beginning. The Navy worried it might one day have to fight in freezing places like the Arctic, so it sent about 4,700 men, 13 ships and 33 aircraft to Antarctica to **train sailors and airmen** and **test ships, planes and equipment** in extreme cold. Other goals were to build a new base, **Little America IV**, on the Ross Ice Shelf, to **photograph and map** huge areas of coastline and unexplored land from the air, to do scientific research, and to give the United States a strong presence in Antarctica at a time when other countries were claiming parts of it.

Byrd was put in charge as **officer in charge of the project**, but the fleet's day-to-day commander was **Rear Admiral Richard Cruzen**. Byrd was then the most famous explorer in America, and his name brought publicity and support. For Byrd personally, it was a chance to return to the place that defined his life. It was his **fourth Antarctic expedition**, and the vast unexplored interior still pulled at him.

## Who he flew with when he "saw" the hidden land

The legend **doesn't give a reliable crew**, because the flight in the legend almost certainly **never happened**. The "secret diary" says that on **February 19, 1947**, Byrd flew from a secret **Arctic** base over the North Pole into a warm hidden land with green valleys, mammoths and a city, and met advanced beings who warned him about atomic weapons. The diary mentions a **radio operator**, but no reliable name for him has been found, and some versions read almost like a solo flight. Historians call the diary a **hoax**: it seems to have started circulating around the late 1970s, about 20 years after Byrd died, and in February 1947 Byrd was actually in **Antarctica**, at the opposite end of the world.

What **really** happened is that on **February 15–16, 1947**, Byrd flew over the **South Pole** in a Navy **R4D** transport, as part of a **two-plane flight**. It was his second flight over the Pole (his first was in 1929), and he **dropped the flags of the United Nations**. The names of the crew on board haven't been confirmed. This real flight is the closest thing to "the flight" of the legend.

## What this means for the film

- The film keeps the legend's date (**February 19, 1947**) but sets the secret flight in **Antarctica**, where Byrd really was. That keeps it consistent with the 1934 opening at Advance Base.
- The crew (Frank, Eddie, Evelyn) is **invented**, so the story isn't "with" any real person.
- **Nimitz and Cruzen** aren't needed as characters. The script uses a fictional admiral (Vice Admiral Prescott) in Cruzen's place, to avoid inventing things about a real person.

## Who was on Operation Highjump

- **Rear Admiral Richard E. Byrd:** officer in charge of the project; in charge of the carrier group.
- **Rear Admiral Richard H. Cruzen:** commander of Task Force 68 and the Central Group (Little America IV).
- **Captain George J. Dufek:** Eastern Group (seaplane tender USS *Pine Island*).
- **Captain Charles A. Bond:** Western Group (icebreaker *Northwind*).
- **Commander William Hawkes:** flew Byrd off the USS *Philippine Sea* on January 29, 1947.
- **Paul Siple:** the Boy Scout on Byrd's 1928 expedition; War Department representative on Highjump.
- **David Bunger:** the pilot who found the ice-free "oasis."
